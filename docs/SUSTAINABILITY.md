# Materials and environmental interpretation

The purpose of the environmental workspace is to connect quality traceability with responsible manufacturing **without** converting a dark-pixel measurement into a fictional CO₂ claim.

## Published aluminium production energy context

The International Aluminium Institute reports 2019 primary-energy demand of **186 GJ per tonne** for global primary aluminium production, from mine to casthouse. Its comparison cites **8.3 GJ per tonne** for secondary production, based on a mix of reported recycling datasets. It presents an approximate **95.5% reduction** in primary energy demand for its comparison.

Original source: https://international-aluminium.org/landing/aluminium-recycling-saves-95-of-the-energy-needed-for-primary-aluminium-production/

**Limitations:** production routes, collection and scrap processing, geographic/electricity mix, system boundaries and scrap allocation vary. This one comparison is *not* a carbon model for a particular metal part. No actual energy saved is derived from a user's image. Avoid equating production energy per tonne with lifetime emissions or assuming recycling is always the preferred step ahead of viable repair or reuse.

For detailed steel inventories, refer to worldsteel's life-cycle inventory releases and declared methodology:
https://worldsteel.org/wider-sustainability/life-cycle-thinking/life-cycle-inventory-data-and-eco-profiles/

## How inspection contributes

1. Use repeated, documented imaging to identify process changes *worth investigating*.
2. Check whether features are optical or preparation artifacts, and whether they change across production batches.
3. If a defect is confirmed, work with materials engineering to evaluate process changes, service-life extension, repair, replacement and the material's end-of-life route.
4. Record alloy/grade identification, process route, recycling content and functional requirements.
5. Quantify real environmental impacts **only** from a suitable product-specific, functionally comparable LCA (ISO 14040/14044 principles).

## Material-family cautions

**Aluminium:** alloys have different recoverability and impurity constraints; recycled feedstock outcomes depend on segregation and process controls.

**Steels:** the steel grade, route, surface coatings and assumed recovery paths matter. Product-specific data are needed rather than a generic metal ranking.

**Titanium:** alloy segregation, aerospace-grade quality requirements and approved repair paths must be documented before circularity assertions.

**Composites:** optical surface imaging does not establish the absence of internal delamination. End-of-life recovery is not equivalent to metal remelting.

**No automatic material certification, repair recommendation, or life-cycle ranking is issued by the application.**
